## Executive Summary
*A two-product residential scheme for two buyer pools*

This paper sets out a mixed residential scheme for the 809 m² THAB site: 26 design-led
residences across a four-level walk-up, built as a cost-efficient brutalist shell and
finished to a distinctive, brand-led interior standard. The scheme deliberately blends
two products — 16 single one-bedroom residences and 10 dual-key residences, each
containing two lockable studios — to address two distinct buyer pools and de-risk the
sell-down.

At adopted prices of $499,000 (one-bedroom) and $599,000 (dual-key), the scheme returns
a 24.7% development margin, 142% return on equity, and supports a residual land value of
$1,761 / m² — above the $1,400 / m² acquisition position. The dual-key product,
generating two income streams, out-yields the one-bedroom (5.6% vs 5.0% gross), so its
higher price is justified by income rather than optimism.

**Adopted scheme: 26 residences, two products — 24.7% margin.**

## The Product
*Two products, one design language, two buyer pools*

The scheme is conceived as a single branded community of 26 residences. The architecture
is intentionally raw — exposed concrete and blockwork in a brutalist idiom — while the
interiors are crafted, warm and highly resolved. Shared "colliding facilities" (laundry,
lounge, office / co-work) deliver a connected-living experience and allow private
footprints to stay compact and efficient.

**Positioning**

- **Two products, deliberately.** 16 single one-bedroom residences (45 m²) reach
  owner-occupiers and investors; 10 dual-key residences (45 m², each two lockable ~20 m²
  studios) target yield-focused investors. Two audiences, halved absorption risk.
- **Income-justified pricing.** A dual-key generates two rental streams (~$640/week,
  ~$33,280/year) versus ~$480/week for a one-bedroom — 33% more income per title. At
  $599,000 it yields 5.6% gross, ahead of the one-bedroom's 5.0%, so the higher price is
  backed by income.
- **Brutalist shell, smart interior.** The structure is the finish — no cladding or
  plasterboard layer — keeping build cost efficient while spend concentrates on joinery,
  integrated storage, smart systems and light.
- **Connected, shared, modern.** Colliding facilities — shared laundry, lounge, office /
  co-work — deliver the connected-living promise and make compact private studios
  genuinely liveable.

**Interior design.** Interior design intent by Studio 11:11 (a Citadel design partner).
The building architecture is to be prepared by a registered architect to be appointed.

**Tenure / operating model.** Amenity-rich, dual-key product can suit
build-to-hold-and-operate as readily as build-to-sell. If sold as individual titles,
ownership and operation of shared facilities must be resolved in the body-corporate
structure. A build-to-hold model should be run alongside build-to-sell.

sensitivity below.

## Investor Yield & Sensitivity
*Why the prices hold, and what moves them*

The scheme's pricing is anchored in income. Both products yield above the ~3–4% typical
of Auckland residential, and the dual-key's two income streams justify its premium.

### Investor yield
| Product | Rent | Annual gross | Gross yield |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1-bed @ $499k | $480/wk | $24,960 | 5.0% |
| Dual-key @ $599k (2 studios) | $640/wk | $33,280 | 5.6% |

### Build-cost sensitivity (the swing variable — computed via model_logic.py)
Build cost is the swing variable — the dual-key fit-out in particular. A $500/m² movement
in the dual-key build rate moves the margin roughly 2–3 points and the land value by
~$400/m². A QS estimate is the single most important figure to confirm.

| Dual-key build $/m² | Blended $/m² | Scheme margin | Land /m² |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3,800 | 3,655 | 25.8% | $1,922 |
| **4,000 (adopted)** | **3,726** | **24.7%** | **$1,761** |
| 4,300 | 3,832 | 23.1% | $1,520 |

### Mix sensitivity (vary the 60/40 split, total 26 — computed)
| Split (of 26) | Margin | Land /m² |
|---|---:|---:|
| 18 × 1-bed + 8 dual-key (69/31) | 24.1% | $1,657 |

More dual-key slightly lifts margin and land value (higher price point) but concentrates
the harder-to-sell investor product. The adopted 60/40 split is the balanced choice.

## Market Assessment
*Demand, supply, and the premium question*

Glen Eden is an established West Auckland suburb 20 minutes from the CBD on the Western
rail line. Median sale price is $823,833 (REINZ, 12 months to June 2026), median 33 days
to sale, 282 dwellings sold. The market is steady and value-led — predominantly first-home
buyers and investors.

### Demand signal
| | |
|---|---|
| Median sale price (all stock, 12 mo) | $823,833 |
| Median days to sale | 33 days |
| Dwellings sold (12 mo) | 282 |
| Median rent (1–2 bed) | $560–620 / week |
| Buyer profile | First-home buyers & investors |

**The premium question — honest read.** Design-led product commands clear premiums in
amenity-rich, transport-central locations (Ponsonby, city fringe, Britomart). Glen Eden is
a solid, affordable, family-and-investor market. The brand, the design and the dual-income
hook improve both price and absorption — but they do not fully override the postcode. This
is the central commercial risk, and the reason the prices must be validated against a real
comparable before commitment.

**Supply & absorption.** New townhouse supply is active (e.g. Creston Group, 39 Brandon
Road, 12 units), absorbed via investor yield guarantees and first-home-buyer deposit
support — evidence of a price-sensitive market. The two-product mix directly addresses
absorption: 16 one-bedrooms to a broad buyer pool and 10 dual-keys to yield investors
spreads the sell-down across audiences rather than concentrating it.

### Recommendation
The mixed scheme is the recommended development concept for the site. It balances return
(24.7% margin, 142% ROE), supports a land value above the acquisition price ($1,761/m² vs
$1,400/m²), and de-risks the sell-down by addressing two buyer pools — owner-occupiers and
yield-focused investors. It is recommended for advancement to concept design, **subject to
the conditions precedent below.**

**Conditions precedent**
1. Registered valuation confirming one-bedroom and dual-key end values.
2. At least one genuine local comparable supporting the $499,000 / $599,000 prices.
3. QS build estimate, with particular focus on the dual-key fit-out rate.
4. Early planner's view on dual-key consent treatment (one dwelling per title, parking,
   and any short-stay use), and confirmation the 2020 consent can be superseded with a
   fresh consent pathway scoped.
5. A build-to-hold operating model run alongside build-to-sell.
6. Body-corporate / shared-facilities ownership and operating structure resolved.
